Price token usage from the LiteLLM table that ships with `@rivus/agent-kit` 0.22.0 (`@rivus/agent-kit/cost/prices`) instead of a committed 15-model snapshot, and upgrade `@rivus/agent-kit` and `@rivus/agent-kit-collab` to 0.22.0 (Effect stays 4.0.5). Prices now update with the kit version, and the chat models of Anthropic, OpenAI, Google Gemini, xAI, DeepSeek, Z.ai, Moonshot and MiniMax are priced, so models such as `glm-5.1`, `grok-4.20`, `kimi-k2.6` and `gemini-3.1-pro-preview` no longer show `n/a`. Costs follow current LiteLLM prices, for example `gpt-5.6-sol` at $4/$20 per million input/output tokens (was $5/$30) and `deepseek-v4-pro` at $1.32/$3.96 (was $0.435/$0.87).

A table key now prices only its own model and its dated ids. `gpt-5.4-mini` and `gpt-5.5-pro`, which took the price of `gpt-5.4` and `gpt-5.5` because their ids contain them, get their own table prices, as do `claude-opus-4-5` through `claude-opus-4-7`, which took the `opus` alias's $15/$75. Suffixed variants such as `gpt-5.5-codex`, `gpt-5.6-sol-high` and `claude-opus-4-8-thinking` keep their model's price through fallback aliases. The `opus` alias, which prices Claude Opus ids the table does not name, charges the current generation's $5/$25 instead of $15/$75; `claude-opus-4-1` keeps its $15/$75. GPT versions the table does not name, such as `gpt-5.7`, show `n/a` instead of GPT-5's price. A partial `usage.pricing` override now takes its missing rates from the model's own price, so `{ "opus": { "input": 9 } }` keeps `claude-opus-4-8`'s $25 output price instead of $75.
